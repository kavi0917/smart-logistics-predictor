# Design Thinking - Improving On-Time Delivery Predictability in Logistics
(23CAP320 Assignment II, Question 2 - and the basis for Questions 3-5)

## 1. Empathize
Who is affected: **logistics operations managers** (own the delivery promise), **dispatchers/drivers** (execute routes), **customers** (wait for goods).
Research methods that would be used: shadowing a control-room shift, interviews with 5-6 managers and dispatchers, reviewing late-delivery complaint
logs and delay reasons, and customer-support call analysis.
Observations / typical findings (used as working assumptions for this prototype):
- Managers learn about a delay **after** the promised time has passed, usually from an angry customer call.
- ETAs given to customers are static estimates that ignore traffic, rain, warehouse queues and the vehicle used.
- Dispatchers know about problems (jam, rain, loading delay) but have no tool that converts them into "this shipment will be late by X minutes".
- Customers care less about speed than about **knowing the truth early**.

## 2. Define
**Persona - Ravi Kumar, Logistics Operations Manager (age 38, regional hub, Chennai)**
- Manages 120-200 shipments a day with 4 dispatchers; measured on on-time delivery % and complaint count.
- Goals: no surprises, early warning, fewer escalations, realistic promises.
- Frustrations: late information, manual phone calls to drivers, blamed for delays he could not see coming.
- Needs: a ranked list of risky shipments, a reason for each risk, and a way to test "what if we switch vehicle / re-route?".
- Tools today: spreadsheets, phone calls, a basic tracking screen.

**Problem statement:** Logistics managers cannot see which shipments are likely to miss their promised time until it is too late to act, so customers
receive unreliable ETAs and complaints rise.

**How Might We...** *How might we predict delivery delays early enough for logistics managers to take corrective action and provide customers with more
reliable delivery times?*

## 3. Ideate
Ideas generated by brainstorming and "crazy eights" (shortlist of 5, top 3 evaluated):
| # | Idea | Value | Effort | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ML-based ETA prediction** from distance, traffic, weather, vehicle, time of day and delays | High | Medium | Selected |
| 2 | **Risk dashboard + proactive alerts** (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH with reasons) | High | Low | Selected |
| 3 | **What-if simulator** to test changing vehicle/traffic/delay and see the new ETA | Medium-High | Low-Medium | Selected |
| 4 | Customer-facing live tracking with SMS notifications | Medium | High (needs paid SMS) | Future |
| 5 | Automatic re-routing using live GPS | High | Very high | Future |

Evaluation used an impact/effort grid: ideas 1-3 give most value for a student-size prototype and complement each other
(predict -> flag -> test options).

## 4. Prototype
**Concept tested first:** a *clickable dashboard prototype* with (a) a shipment list showing predicted ETA and risk badge and (b) a shipment detail
page that lists the reasons for the risk. Steps:
1. Paper sketches / low-fidelity wireframes of the dashboard, alerts list and detail page.
2. Medium fidelity: React screens with sample data to walk managers through it.
3. **Working prototype (this repository):** React + FastAPI + PostgreSQL + a trained model, so the numbers shown are real calculations.

## 5. Test
| What we test | How | Success measure |
|---|---|---|
| Understandability of risk + reasons | 5 managers, think-aloud on 3 tasks ("find the riskiest shipment", "why is L001 risky?", "what if traffic drops?") | >= 4 of 5 complete tasks without help |
| Usefulness of alerts | Compare alert list with shipments that really ran late (history) | Most late shipments flagged before delivery |
| Prediction quality | MAE / RMSE and "on-time call accuracy" in the Analytics page | MAE small relative to promised times |
| Usability | SUS questionnaire, observe confusion points | SUS >= 70 |
Iteration: feedback feeds the product backlog (see SCRUM_PLAN.md) - e.g. add filters, simpler wording, clearer reason labels.
Note: in this assignment the user tests are *planned*; only automated tests and the prediction-accuracy analytics were actually run.
